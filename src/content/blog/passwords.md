### Heads Up
Protecting your devices and accounts is like locking the front door. Choosing a weak password is like hiding the key under the doormat.

### At a Glance
As kids begin using more technology, they'll start creating passwords for school accounts, Chromebooks, educational websites, and eventually their first cell phone.

Learning how to create a good password early is one of the easiest ways to stay safe online. A strong password helps keep your information private and prevents other people from accessing your accounts.

### The Threat
Not everyone who wants access to an account is a stranger on the internet. Sometimes it's a classmate trying to guess a password, a bully looking to cause trouble, or someone who simply shouldn't have access.

Many people choose passwords based on things that are easy to remember, such as:

- Their name
- A pet's name
- Their favorite sports team
- Their school mascot
- A birthday or graduation year

The problem is that these are often the first things someone will guess.

### Outsmart It
Creating a good password doesn't have to be difficult.

When creating a password:

- Don't use your name, birthday, or other personal information.
- Avoid common words like "password" or "123456."
- Use uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters when possible.
- Use a different password for different accounts.
- Never share your password with friends.

As you get older and create more online accounts, you'll want even stronger passwords. That's why cybersecurity professionals recommend using **passphrases**, which are longer, easier to remember, and much harder to guess.

#### EXTRA CONTENT
##### How Strong Is Your Password?

Want to see how strong your password is?

Visit: [Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com/password-strength/)

As you type a password, the website estimates how long it would take a computer to crack it. It's a fun way to see how making small changes—like adding more characters or creating a longer password—can make it much stronger.

For most kids, it's okay if your password is rated **"Weak"** when you're first learning. Try improving it until you reach **"Good"** or better.

**Your password is not sent to the website.** The password strength check is performed locally in your web browser, so what you type never leaves your device.

**Tip:** Instead of entering a real password, create a practice password to see how different combinations affect its strength.
